## The Executive Addiction Pattern

High-performing leaders are not more likely to develop substance use disorders because they lack willpower. They are more likely because the environment rewards the behavior. Alcohol closes deals, signals confidence, and manages the cortisol load of decisions that ripple across hundreds of lives. Stimulants extend focus through exhaustion. Benzodiazepines quiet the anxiety that builds behind the results. Sleep medication becomes a necessity when adrenaline makes rest impossible.

None of it starts as addiction. It starts as a tool — until the tool runs the person.

Research supports this: high-responsibility roles are associated with elevated rates of hazardous alcohol use, particularly in cultures where social drinking is normalized as part of leadership. A Harvard Business Review analysis found that senior leaders routinely underestimate their own drinking relative to peers, precisely because peer consumption is elevated. The benchmark shifts upward, and so does the problem.

Our dual-diagnosis program treats what drove the substance use alongside the substance use itself: performance anxiety, perfectionism, the identity tied to outcome, and the inability to ask for help that got reinforced for decades by a culture that treated it as weakness.
