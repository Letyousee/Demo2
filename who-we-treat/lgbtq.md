## Understanding the LGBTQ+ Substance Use Risk

The connection between LGBTQ+ identity and higher rates of substance use is well-documented — and it has nothing to do with who you are. It has everything to do with what researchers call the minority stress model: the chronic, cumulative burden of navigating discrimination, stigma, family rejection, and the pressure to conceal your identity.

LGBTQ+ adults are more than twice as likely as non-LGBTQ+ adults to have a substance use disorder (SAMHSA, 2020). Among LGBTQ+ youth, the gap is wider still: a 2021 Trevor Project survey found that 52 percent of LGBTQ+ young people seriously considered suicide in the past year, with substance use frequently part of that picture.

Alcohol is often used to manage social anxiety and the stress of non-affirming environments. Stimulants and party drugs are common in specific community settings. Prescription medications become a way to manage chronic anxiety and depression that go untreated. What connects all of it is a person trying to cope with a world that has often been unkind to them.

Effective LGBTQ+ rehab does not ignore this history. It starts there.
